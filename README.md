## About

I am an **Electronics & Communication Engineering student** building toward embedded systems, digital electronics, and hardware engineering. I enjoy turning an engineering idea into a demonstrable system—working across microcontrollers, sensors, digital logic, simulation, and the software that helps make hardware useful.

I am currently developing practical projects with **Arduino**, **ESP32**, **C/C++**, and **Python**, while strengthening my understanding of digital logic, hardware–software integration, and sensor-based systems. I am also exploring PCB design and see **Verilog / FPGA** as an important next learning area. When it serves an engineering problem, I am interested in applying computer vision and machine-learning fundamentals at the edge.

My aim is to build carefully, test what I make, and grow through projects—not simply collect certifications.

## Featured Projects

<details>
<summary><strong>BELTCORE — Joint-Centric Multimodal Degradation Intelligence for Conveyor Belt Splices</strong></summary>

<br />

BELTCORE is a **student prototype / academic engineering project** exploring condition-to-action monitoring for conveyor-belt splices. The concept brings together RGB vision, vibration, acoustic and temperature / thermal sensing, plus speed and load context to support joint identification, a Joint Health Index, and a Digital Joint Passport. It is intended as an experimental edge-processing system—not an industrial deployment.

| Category | Details |
|---|---|
| Stack | Multimodal sensing, RGB vision, vibration, acoustic sensing, thermal / temperature sensing, edge-processing concepts |
| Scale | Student prototype / academic engineering project |
| Performance | No deployment or performance metrics claimed |
| Security | Not applicable at prototype stage; data handling and device security remain design considerations |
| Impact | Explores practical, traceable monitoring of splice condition to inform maintenance decisions |
| Repository | Project repository not yet public |

The engineering direction is joint-centric rather than generic belt monitoring: each observed splice can be associated with condition history and a decision-support record while the project evaluates how multiple signals complement one another.

</details>
